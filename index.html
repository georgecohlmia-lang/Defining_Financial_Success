Redefining Financial Success in Year 1–2

In corporate, success had a number on it before the year started. In year one of your own thing, nobody hands you that slide. This worksheet builds the scorecard that actually matters — the one you'll answer every Friday for ten minutes, while you're figuring out what your business really is.

How to use this worksheet: The centerpiece is the Friday Scorecard in Part 2. Fill it in honestly, then keep it open every week. Your answers save automatically.
Part 1

The Wrong Scoreboard

Your corporate metrics measured whether a mature engine was running efficiently. In year one, you don't have an engine yet — you have a hypothesis. Start by naming which metrics you're still anchored to, even though they tell you almost nothing useful right now.

Revenue growth rate Year-over-year revenue % — designed to compare a mature business to itself
Gross margin % Useful when you have repeatable delivery — not when you're still figuring it out
Operating margin / EBITDA Grades the engine. You're sketching, not driving.
Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC) Requires a stable acquisition channel. You don't have one yet.
Payback period Calculated against assumptions you'll change three times this year
Part 2

The Friday Scorecard — Four Questions That Actually Matter

Four questions, every Friday, ten minutes. None of them are revenue questions. All of them are progress questions. Fill in your answers for this past week to start the practice now.

1 Is the team intact?
The team is the one thing on a startup balance sheet you cannot rebuild quickly when it leaves.
This week:
If someone left or is wavering — why, and is it a signal?
If everyone's here and engaged, write what kept them there this week.
2 Closer to making it through the next quarter — or further away?
Be specific. Cash position, not feeling. Survival is the strategy that makes every other strategy possible.
This week:
Cash position now and runway in months — be specific.
Numbers, not vibes. If you can't say it precisely, that's the first thing to fix this weekend.
3 Did I take any actions this week that reduced my optionality?
Optionality is the founder's most valuable currency. Also the easiest to spend without noticing.
This week:
If yes — what was it, and was it worth it?
A long contract. A premature hire. An exclusive partnership. A funding term. Be honest about whether the trade was conscious.
4 Did anything happen this week that felt like the playbook getting traction?
A repeat customer. A pitch that landed. A process that finally worked. Even one is enough to write down.
This week:
What was it? Be specific.
If nothing — what's one experiment you could run next week to create the chance for traction?
